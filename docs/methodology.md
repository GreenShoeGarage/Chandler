# Methodology

CHANDLER distinguishes evidence of use from engineering recommendation.

## Observed Commonness

The target published weighting is independent-project presence (50 percent), project-category breadth (20 percent), independent project-family count (15 percent), recency (10 percent), and valid regional diversity (5 percent). If regional data is unavailable, its weight must be redistributed and documented. Project presence is primary; raw quantity is secondary.

## Prototype Utility

The target published weighting is sourcing resilience (25 percent), interchangeability (20 percent), ease of fabrication and assembly (20 percent), documentation and Computer-Aided Design or Electronic Design Automation assets (15 percent), cost suitability (10 percent), and reuse potential (10 percent).

Every published score includes factor values, evidence, review date, confidence, and the applicable catalog release. The bundled catalog uses demonstration scores only.

## Census controls

Projects are stratified across machines, robotics, workshop fixtures, home automation, scientific instruments, art, repair and accessibility, and environmental equipment. Forks, repeated revisions, rebranded kits, mirrors, and templates share a project-family identifier so they cannot dominate prevalence.
